Overview

Lin Heung Tea House is a Cantonese tea house known for "one cup, two pieces". Its brand traces back to the Guangzhou Lin Heung of 1889, while the Hong Kong shop opened in Central in 1927. It is no longer at its old Wellington Street site: in May 2026 it moved to 249–253 Des Voeux Road Central in Sheung Wan.

It is not a tea-only room but a tea house. Guests order tea first, then wait for a dim sum trolley to pass and choose har gow, siu mai, char siu bao, chicken feet, ma lai go and other items. Tea, dim sum, a noisy hall and shared tables are all part of the format.

History

The Guangzhou Lin Heung opened in 1889 as a tea house centred on pastries and tea. In 1927 the Hong Kong Lin Heung opened on Queen's Road Central, bringing the Guangzhou tea-house model to Hong Kong. In 1996 the Hong Kong shop moved to 160–164 Wellington Street, the address that became the last memory of Lin Heung for many visitors.

On 9 August 2022 the Wellington Street shop closed because of lease and operating problems. In April 2024 Lin Heung reopened at the same site, still promoting traditional dim sum trolleys and covered-bowl tea. On 11 May 2026 it moved again, to 249–253 Des Voeux Road Central, Sheung Wan, occupying the basement to second floor of Tung Ning Building.

This history matters: Lin Heung is not a shop that has never moved or never closed. Its continuity comes from the brand, the dim sum and the tea-house format, not from one building. Any address claim must separate the 1996 Wellington Street site, the 2024 reopening there and the 2026 Sheung Wan move.

Tea and space

Tea at Lin Heung centres on pu'er, jasmine and oolong, served in covered bowls or teapots. The tea house keeps the old "tea money" and dim sum card system: tea is opened first, the trolley stamps the card as dishes are chosen, and the bill is calculated from the plates and tea charge. The process is slower than scanning a QR code, but it is the rhythm of the old tea house.

The dim sum trolley is the part people remember. Staff push steamers through the hall; guests look at the trolley, choose dishes and sometimes rush a freshly arrived batch of char siu bao or har gow. Shared tables are normal: at lunchtime, sitting at a round table with strangers is not unusual.

The new Sheung Wan branch occupies the basement to second floor of Tung Ning Building. It is larger than the old shop but keeps the trolley, covered-bowl tea and the large hall layout. It is not a museum reconstruction; it moves the old tea-house way of operating into a new address. Whether it feels "old enough" depends on the trolleys and the tea, not on the wall decoration.

Visiting

Address: basement to second floor, Tung Ning Building, 249–253 Des Voeux Road Central, Sheung Wan, Hong Kong. Status: operating; reopened at the new address on 11 May 2026. Checked on 25 November 2026.

Morning tea and lunch are usually the busiest periods. Current hours, queueing arrangements and dim sum prices should be confirmed with the shop on the day. This is a tea house, not an independent teahouse; plan for tea plus dim sum, and expect shared tables and a wait.

Source boundaries

This page uses the Chinese Wikipedia entry on Hong Kong Lin Heung, the China News Service report of the May 2026 move and Time Out Hong Kong's report on the new branch to support the 1889 Guangzhou origin, the 1927 Hong Kong opening, the 1996 Wellington Street move, the August 2022 closure, the April 2024 reopening, the May 2026 Sheung Wan move, the Tung Ning Building address and the trolley/covered-bowl tea format.

Older copy about a film star shooting a scene at Lin Heung, a regular customer's fixed seat, a claim that Lin Heung "never closed" and the precise founding year of a particular dim sum lacks a checkable source and is not repeated. The 2022 closure and the 2024 reopening are two separate operating events and should not be merged into "always open".
