Overview

Café Batavia stands at 14 Pintu Besar Utara in Jakarta’s old town, Kota Tua, facing Fatahillah Square. It occupies an early nineteenth-century colonial building and opened as a restaurant in 1993 after a restoration by Graham James. This is not a traditional teahouse but a composite of colonial architecture, Dutch-Indonesian food and old-town memory.

The building has two floors: a long ground-floor hall and bar, and the Grand Salon above, with timber shutters facing the square. Stucco, shutters and ironwork keep the Dutch colonial surface. The surrounding quarter was the administrative and commercial core of Dutch Batavia, and the town hall, canals, warehouses and churches nearby still frame the approach to the café. The façade is low and wide, with taller first-floor windows, a pediment over the entrance and ironwork details typical of Dutch colonial public architecture.

History

Sources differ on the building date: the official site says 1805, Wikipedia says the 1830s. It began as a residence for senior VOC officials and later served as administrative offices, a gallery and a warehouse. The block around it was shaped by the city’s seventeenth- and eighteenth-century expansion and canal works; after the building took its early nineteenth-century form it served first as an officials’ residence, then as offices and storage. During the 1990s revival of the old town it was chosen for conversion into a restaurant, with the restoration keeping stucco, shutters and timber details while adding modern lighting and kitchen systems. Graham James led the restoration in 1992–93, and the café opened in 1993.

Food and space

The menu is Dutch-Indonesian and Indonesian: rijsttafel, satay, curries, fried banana, coffee and tea. The first-floor Grand Salon is the main room, where shutters, old photographs, mirrors and pendant lights produce a restored-old-world atmosphere. It suits looking onto the square and a slow meal rather than a quiet tea sitting.

Visiting

Address: Jl. Pintu Besar Utara No. 14, Kota Tua, Jakarta Barat. Hours are usually 8.00–24.00 daily; check the official page for the day. Weekends and holiday afternoons are busier, so midday is best avoided.

Source boundary

The official site gives 1805 and Wikipedia gives the 1830s; this page keeps both rather than forcing one. 1993 is the café’s opening year, not the building’s construction date. Old details about a governor-general working here or a film being shot on site have no reliable source and are omitted.
