Overview

Fortnum & Mason occupies 181 Piccadilly in London, a grocery business that became one of the city's best-known department stores. On its fourth floor is the Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon, opened by Elizabeth II in March 2012 and named for the diamond jubilee of her accession.

The "tea salon" is not an independent street teahouse. It is a formal tea room inside a department store: tea, food and gifts are sold downstairs, while upstairs a white-tablecloth tea service with piano accompaniment takes place. The right way to understand it is "a full English afternoon tea inside a historic London department store", not "a tea shop where you buy a packet of leaves".

History

William Fortnum and Hugh Mason founded the business in London in 1707. The Fortnum family had court connections, and the firm expanded from groceries and tea into a broader food, gift and dining business. The Piccadilly flagship took shape in the nineteenth century and remains a landmark in St James's.

The Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon was created for the diamond jubilee of Elizabeth II's accession and opened on the fourth floor in March 2012. The official account records that the Queen attended the opening. That date shows it is not a tea room "continuing since 1707"; it is a twenty-first-century reorganisation of the store's tea experience.

Fortnum & Mason has long blended and sold tea, which allows the salon to connect the tea sold downstairs with the tea served at the table. The firm also holds a history of Royal Warrants, but this page treats that as commercial history, not as evidence of a monarch's private daily preference.

Tea and space

The salon occupies the fourth floor. Visitors pass through the store's staircases and displays before entering the tea room. Inside, pale walls, formal tables and a piano set the pace: choose a tea, wait for the three-tier stand, and listen to music in between.

The menu covers classic afternoon tea, a savoury afternoon tea, a fuller high tea, and vegetarian and gluten-free options. The tea list is built around Fortnum's own blends, with Earl Grey, breakfast tea and Darjeeling among the usual choices; the exact list changes with the season. The three-tier stand remains central: sandwiches, scones with clotted cream, and pastries.

It does not pursue the lighter, casual style of a modern tea bar. It keeps the formality of a full sitting. Ordering only a pot of tea would miss the point; the salon is designed for a complete tea service.

Visiting

Address: 181 Piccadilly, St James's, London W1A 1ER, United Kingdom. Status: operating; the tea salon requires a booking. Checked on 25 November 2026.

Times, prices, child policy and dress expectations should be taken from the Fortnum & Mason official page. The salon is on the fourth floor of the store, so the route differs from a street-level teahouse. Allow at least ninety minutes and do not compress the visit into a tight schedule.

Source boundaries

This page uses the official Fortnum & Mason tea-salon page, the official careers/about page and the Wikipedia department-store history entry to support the 1707 founding, 181 Piccadilly, the fourth-floor salon, the March 2012 opening, the Tearista and piano, and the classic/high-tea/vegetarian/gluten-free options.

Older copy about a particular royal person's regular tea, a "world's best" award for the salon, a specific price in a given year and a private royal tea list lacks a checkable source and is not repeated. A Royal Warrant is a fact of commercial history; it does not mean that a monarch personally endorsed a product.
