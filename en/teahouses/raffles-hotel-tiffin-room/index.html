Overview

Tiffin Room sits inside Raffles Hotel Singapore at 1 Beach Road. Raffles Hotel was founded by the Sarkies brothers in 1887; the Tiffin Room business dates to a Tiffin Room opened in Commercial Square in May 1892, moved into the hotel after its 1910 expansion and took its present name in 1976. It is a North Indian dining room, not a standalone tea salon.

“Tiffin” in colonial India and Southeast Asia meant a between-meals snack or a lunch box; the room takes its name from that eating habit, not from a particular tea.

History

In 1887 the Sarkies brothers took over Seaside House on Beach Road and turned it into Raffles Hotel. In May 1892 Tiffin Room opened as a separate business in Commercial Square, today’s Raffles Place; after the hotel’s 1910 expansion it moved to its present site. In 1976 the hotel formally named the restaurant Tiffin Room.

A major restoration of Raffles Hotel was completed in 2019, and Tiffin Room was renewed with it. Its “since 1892” refers to the business, not to the present room having existed since that year.

Food and space

Tiffin Room cooks North Indian food: tandoor oven, copper tiffin boxes, spices ground at the table, curries and breads. Tea and coffee are served, but the core is a meal rather than afternoon tea. The room is on the ground floor, next to timber shutters, white colonnades and the tropical courtyard; diners look out toward the hotel’s palms and fountain.

Visiting

Address: 1 Beach Road, Singapore 189673. Hours and menu follow the Raffles Hotel website; dinner bookings are advised, while lunch and afternoon periods usually accept walk-ins. Esplanade and City Hall are the nearest MRT stations.

Source boundary

“Since 1892” marks the start of the Tiffin Room business, not the construction date of the present room; this page separates 1892, 1910 and 1976. Old claims about a “royal afternoon tea” or a novelist writing on the premises are not supported across the official site and Wikipedia and are omitted. Tiffin Room is a dining room, not a standalone tea salon, and the title and text keep that distinction.
