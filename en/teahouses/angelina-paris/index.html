Overview

Angelina stands at 226 Rue de Rivoli in the first arrondissement, facing the Tuileries Garden. It was founded in 1903 by the Austrian confectioner Antoine Rumpelmayer as a Belle Époque salon de thé and pâtisserie. Most visitors queue for two things: the thick hot chocolate L’Africain and the chestnut Mont-Blanc.

This is not a teahouse in the narrow sense. It is a salon de thé where tea, coffee, hot chocolate, pastries and light meals share one menu, and the room is as much the attraction as the food.

History

Rumpelmayer opened the salon under the arcades of Rue de Rivoli in 1903. His son René and other family members continued the business, and the house took the name Angelina from a female family member; Wikipedia and the brand site differ slightly on whether this was a daughter or daughter-in-law, so this page keeps only the family-name layer.

The main salon was designed by the architect Édouard-Jean Niermans, with marble, mirrors, painted panels and warm woodwork in a Belle Époque idiom. In the first half of the twentieth century it became a meeting place for Parisian society, fashion and literature; Wikipedia records Marcel Proust and Coco Chanel as regulars. The brand now has branches in France and abroad, but the Rivoli address remains the one usually called the original.

Tea and space

Angelina’s signature is L’Africain, a hot chocolate served in a small pitcher and thick enough to feel like a liquid dessert, usually with a bowl of whipped cream. The Mont-Blanc is chestnut cream piped into fine strands over meringue and Chantilly; Paris-Brest, millefeuille, macarons and seasonal pastries fill out the counter. Tea and coffee are on the menu too, but most guests come for the chocolate and pastry.

The room is Belle Époque in manner: mirrors, painted panels, marble tables, warm light and not much space between tables. It suits a full dessert sitting rather than quiet laptop work. The takeaway counter at the entrance sells Mont-Blanc and chocolate and can shorten the wait.

Visiting

Address: 226 Rue de Rivoli, 75001 Paris. The official and branch pages usually list daily hours of 8.00–19.00, sometimes longer at weekends; check the day’s page. The Rivoli salon works mainly by walk-in queue and does not show a standard online booking entry, so mid-morning or later afternoon is usually easier. Tuileries is the nearest metro station.

Source boundary

This page uses only what the brand site, the Rivoli branch page and Wikipedia can support: the 1903 founding, Antoine Rumpelmayer, the Niermans salon, L’Africain and Mont-Blanc, and the Proust/Chanel record. Old claims that it has “the best hot chocolate in Paris”, won a particular award in a particular year, or never changed its Mont-Blanc recipe do not form a checkable chain and are omitted. Prices and hours change and are not fixed here.
