What it is

Ippodo Tea is a long-established Japanese tea shop on Teramachi-dori in Kyoto, founded in 1717. It sells matcha, gyokuro, sencha, bancha and teaware, and has a tea room, Kiki, in the Kyoto main store where guests can order tea and experience Japanese tea preparation. It is a tea shop with a tea room, not a restaurant serving full meals.

Location and visiting

The Kyoto main store is at 52 Tokiwagi-cho, Teramachi-dori Nijo-agaru, Nakagyo-ku, Kyoto, near Kyoto City Hall and the Teramachi shopping street. Operating status was checked on 2026-10-30; check the official website for current hours, last orders in the tea room, reservations and closing days. Tea-room seats are limited, so confirm in busy seasons.

Tea and experience

Ippodo handles Uji-style matcha, gyokuro, sencha and bancha, and sells tea bowls, whisks and other teaware. The Kiki tea room offers matcha, sencha and other teas for tasting, useful for comparing tea types or experiencing Japanese tea preparation. Menus, prices and languages vary; “300 years, one thing” is brand narrative, not an audited operating statistic.

History and boundaries

Ippodo was founded by the Omi merchant Watanabe Ihei in 1717, initially selling tea, teaware and ceramics; it later received the name “Ippodo”, meaning “wholeheartedly protect tea”. It is part of Kyoto’s Japanese-tea commercial history, but “most famous in Kyoto” and “must visit” are market evaluations. Dates, tea-school relationships and celebrity stories in old records should be checked against official history and Kyoto tea-industry sources.

Common misconceptions

  • Ippodo does not sell only matcha. It also sells gyokuro, sencha, bancha and teaware.
  • The tea room is not a restaurant. It focuses on tea and teaware, not full meals.
  • “300 years” is the founding date, not the age of every product. Tea is produced in the present; brand history is not tea vintage.
  • Hours change. Check the official website, especially the tea room’s last order time.

Ordering and buying notes

First-time visitors can start with sencha or gyokuro before comparing matcha; the tea room usually serves by pot or bowl with brief brewing guidance. Seasonal teas, sweets and gift sets change, and the website sometimes marks items as sold out; check stock in advance for a specific vintage or garden. Seating is limited and queues are common on busy afternoons, so split shopping and tasting into separate stops if time is tight.
