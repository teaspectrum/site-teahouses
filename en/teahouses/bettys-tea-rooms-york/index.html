Overview

Bettys Café Tea Rooms stands at 6-8 St Helen’s Square in York and is one of the city’s most recognisable tea rooms. It belongs to the Bettys family business founded in Harrogate in 1919; the York branch opened in 1937. Unlike a hotel tea service, this is a café-and-shop operation: breakfast, lunch and afternoon tea are served, and bread, cakes and tea are sold at the counter.

The York building has a brick and stone elevation, while the interior is known for curved windows, wood panelling and Art Deco lines. Its design was inspired by the Cunard liner RMS Queen Mary, a point made by Bettys’ own history and by the York visit page.

History

The founder, Frederick Belmont, was born Fritz Bützer in Switzerland and trained as a baker, confectioner and chocolatier. He arrived in England in 1907, drifted to Yorkshire, worked in a Swiss confectionery in Harrogate and later adopted an English name. On 17 July 1919 he opened the first Bettys in Harrogate; a bakery followed in 1922, making further branches possible.

In 1936 Belmont travelled on the maiden voyage of the Queen Mary. Impressed by the ship’s interiors, he engaged the liner’s designers and craftsmen to convert an old furniture shop on St Helen’s Square. Bettys York opened on 1 June 1937 and became the largest branch; the Belmont Room kept the cabin-like woodwork and mirrors. During the Second World War the basement Bettys Bar became a gathering place for Allied airmen stationed around York, and the signed Bettys Mirror remains on display.

In 1962 Bettys merged with Taylors of Harrogate, a tea and coffee business founded in 1886, creating the Bettys and Taylors group. That helps explain why the cafés sell both fresh baking and Yorkshire Tea.

Tea and space

The York site combines a café, a shop and the upstairs Belmont Room. The everyday menu covers breakfast, lunch and afternoon tea; the bookable afternoon tea is served in the Belmont Room with a three-tier stand and a more formal table setting. Signature items include the Yorkshire curd tart, the Fat Rascal (a fruit-and-almond rock cake) and a rotating range of cakes. The tea list leans on Yorkshire Tea and house blends, and the company holds more than 600 teapots; the York café displays a Queen Mary-shaped teapot.

This is a northern English café tea room rather than a hotel salon: there are queues, local regulars, a takeaway counter and visitors who come specifically for a full afternoon tea. The appeal is consistency and daily use, not ceremonial grandeur.

Visiting

Address: 6-8 St Helen’s Square, York YO1 8QP. Hours follow the official page and usually run from morning into the late afternoon, with longer Thursday-to-Saturday hours. Bookable afternoon tea is in the Belmont Room; ordinary café seating is by arrival. The shop is separate from the café, so it is easy to eat first and buy cakes afterwards.

Source boundary

The official York page says “since 1936”, while the company history and local records give an opening date of 1 June 1937. This page treats 1936 as the remodelling and preparation year and 1937 as the formal opening, and keeps the discrepancy visible. Old claims about a royal warrant or being “York’s best tea room” are not supported across the official site and Wikipedia and are omitted. Menus and prices change by season and are not fixed here.
