United Kingdom

Afternoon Tea at The Ritz London

The Ritz London opened at 150 Piccadilly in 1906 and serves afternoon tea in the Palm Court. Its Louis XVI-style room, three-tier stand, roughly 20 loose-leaf teas and formal dress code make it a hotel ritual rather than a standalone teahouse.

France

Angelina, Rue de Rivoli, Paris

Angelina opened on Rue de Rivoli in 1903 as a Belle Époque salon de thé and pâtisserie. Its signatures are the thick L’Africain hot chocolate and the chestnut Mont-Blanc; the Niermans-designed room serves tea, coffee and pastries on one menu.

United Kingdom

Bettys Café Tea Rooms, York

Bettys York opened on St Helen’s Square in 1937. It combines a Swiss confectioner’s baking tradition, Yorkshire tea and Queen Mary-inspired interiors in one café tea room, with everyday counter service and bookable afternoon tea in the Belmont Room.

Indonesia

Café Batavia, Jakarta

Café Batavia occupies an early nineteenth-century Dutch colonial building in Jakarta’s old town and opened in 1993 after a restoration by Graham James. It is known for Dutch-Indonesian cooking, the first-floor Grand Salon and shutters facing Fatahillah Square, not for a traditional tea service.

Austria

Café Central

A traditional Viennese coffee house at Herrengasse 14, opened in 1876 on the ground floor of Palais Ferstel. It is known for its late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century intellectual circles, but it is a coffee house first and a tea stop second.

Spain

Café Gijón, Madrid

Café Gijón opened on Madrid’s Paseo de Recoletos in 1888 and became a symbol of Spanish literary tertulias. The Café Gijón novel prize was founded there in 1949; after a sale in late 2025 it reopened in April 2026 as Capucchino Grand Café Gijón, keeping the address and historic room.

Austria

Café Sacher

The café inside Hotel Sacher, opposite the Vienna State Opera, built around the Original Sacher-Torte. Read it in three layers: the 1832 creation story, the hotel history from 1876, and the cake dispute around 1963.

Czechia

Café Slavia, Prague

Café Slavia stands at Národní 8 in Prague, facing the National Theatre. A café opened on the site in 1881, it reopened as Slavia in 1884 and was remodelled in Art Deco style in 1931–32; it is a Czech literary and dissident meeting place rather than a specialised teahouse.

Egypt

El Fishawy Café

An old café in Cairo’s Khan el-Khalili market, known for mirrors, mint tea and literary memory; read its founding date and famous visitors as layered claims, not a single verified story.

Canada

Fairmont Empress Afternoon Tea (Victoria)

The Fairmont Empress sits on Victoria's Inner Harbour. Opened by the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1908, it is a National Historic Site with 431 rooms, and its afternoon tea is a hotel experience rather than a standalone teahouse.

United Kingdom

Fortnum & Mason Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon (London)

Fortnum & Mason was founded in 1707, and its Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon on the fourth floor of the Piccadilly flagship was opened by Elizabeth II in March 2012. The salon combines the department store's tea retail, a formal tea service and live piano in one floor, making it an unusual "tea salon within a department store".

China · Sichuan

Guanyinge Old Teahouse, Peng Town

A century-old tea house on Mashi Ba Street in Peng Town, Shuangliu, Chengdu, housed in a former Qing Guanyin shrine; its tiger stove, trodden-earth floor, bamboo chairs and gaiwan tea remain in daily use, and its restoration won a 2024 UNESCO Asia-Pacific heritage award.

China · Sichuan

Heming Teahouse

A century-old tea house in Chengdu’s People’s Park, founded in 1923 and known for bamboo chairs, low tables, gaiwan tea, long-spout copper kettles and the slow everyday life of a public park.

China · Shanghai

Huxinting Teahouse

Huxinting Teahouse sits in the middle of the lotus pond at Yu Garden’s Nine-Bend Bridge in Shanghai, one of the city’s oldest teahouses. The building dates to 1784 and became a teahouse in 1855; after restoration from 2023 to 2025, it reopened to the public on 10 July 2025.

Japan

Ippodo Tea

Ippodo Tea was founded in 1717 and is a long-established Japanese tea shop on Teramachi-dori in Kyoto. It sells matcha, gyokuro and sencha and has a tea room, Kiki, for tasting. It is a tea shop with a tea room, not a full-service restaurant.

China · Xinjiang

Kashgar Old Tea House

A century-old tea house at the junction of Wusitangboyi and Kumudai’erwaza roads in Kashgar’s old town, where fu brick tea, clear tea, medicinal infusions, naan, earthen platforms and muqam music share one room.

France

Ladurée

A Paris pastry and tea-room house at 16 Rue Royale, founded in 1862 by Louis Ernest Ladurée. Its core is French pastry, macarons and salon-style tea, not specialist tea retail.

China · Beijing

Lao She Teahouse

Lao She Teahouse is on Qianmen West Street in Beijing, opened in 1988 and named after the writer Lao She. It combines tea, Beijing snacks and stage performances such as quyi, Peking opera and acrobatics; it is a modern cultural teahouse, not the original site of Lao She’s fictional teahouse.

China

Lin Heung Tea House (Hong Kong)

Hong Kong's Lin Heung Tea House traces its brand to the Guangzhou Lin Heung of 1889 and opened in Central in 1927. It moved to Wellington Street in 1996, closed in August 2022, reopened in April 2024 and moved to 249–253 Des Voeux Road Central in Sheung Wan in May 2026, keeping its trolley dim sum, covered-bowl tea and "one cup, two pieces" service.

China · Hong Kong

Luk Yu Teahouse

A long-running Cantonese tea house in Central, Hong Kong, founded in 1933 and moved to Stanley Street in 1976; read it through rosewood furniture, calligraphy, old signage and the rhythm of tea and dim sum.

France

Mariage Frères

A Paris tea importer and tea house founded in 1854, which only opened to retail in 1984; read the brand story, the wholesale history and today’s tea menu as separate layers.

South Africa

Mount Nelson Afternoon Tea (Cape Town)

The Mount Nelson Hotel opened in Cape Town in 1899, at the foot of Table Mountain in the Gardens district. Its afternoon tea is served in the Lounge and the Windsor Table with more than 60 loose-leaf teas and a three-tier stand; it was introduced in November 1989 as a hotel dining tradition, not as an independent teahouse.

Hungary

New York Café, Budapest

New York Café opened in 1894 on the ground floor of Budapest’s New York Palace and became a centre of Hungarian literary coffee-house culture. It is a coffee house rather than a specialised teahouse; marble columns, frescoes, chandeliers and live music define the room, restored in 2006.

Russia

Perlov Tea House (Moscow)

The Perlov Tea House at 19 Myasnitskaya Street, Moscow, was built by Roman Klein in 1890–1893 and refaced in a Chinese style in 1895–1896. Once a major imperial tea merchant house, it was nationalised after 1917 and, after a restoration begun in 1998 and lasting about twelve years, reopened in 2012 as a tea and coffee shop.

China

Tao Tao Ju (Guangzhou)

Tao Tao Ju opened on Dishifu Road in Guangzhou's Xiguan district in 1880 and became a representative Cantonese tea house of the "one cup, two pieces" tradition. After a 1925 expansion, a late-twentieth-century lease, a 2018 recovery and a 2022 reopening, it is now operated by the Guangzhou Restaurant Group at its original Dishifu Road address.

United Kingdom

The Mackintosh Tearooms (Willow Tea Rooms)

The tea rooms at 217 Sauchiehall Street, Glasgow, were opened by Catherine Cranston in 1903, with interiors by Charles Rennie Mackintosh and Margaret Macdonald. Restored and reopened in 2018, taken over by the National Trust for Scotland in 2024 and renamed The Mackintosh Tearooms in 2026.

United Kingdom

The Pump Room (Bath)

Bath's Pump Room is a late-eighteenth-century Georgian social building, now run by Searcys as a restaurant and tea room. It combines a mineral spring, a Corinthian-columned hall and English afternoon tea in one room, which makes it different from an ordinary street teahouse.

Morocco

The tea rooms at La Mamounia

Two tea rooms inside a Marrakech palace hotel, read through an eighteenth- century garden, a railway-era building, and the meeting of Moroccan craft and Art Deco.

Singapore

Tiffin Room at Raffles Hotel Singapore

Tiffin Room sits inside Raffles Hotel Singapore, with a business history going back to 1892, a move into the hotel in 1910 and its present name from 1976. It is a North Indian dining room known for tandoor cooking, copper tiffin boxes and tableside spice grinding, not a standalone tea salon.

United Kingdom

Twinings Tea Shop (216 Strand)

The Twinings shop at 216 Strand in London, opened by Thomas Twining in 1706 and still trading at the same address. It is a tea retailer and brand flagship, not a traditional English afternoon-tea salon.

China · Taiwan

Wistaria Tea House

A Japanese-era wooden house in Taipei, converted into a tea house in 1981 and designated a city historical site in 1997; closed since December 2024 for a multi-year restoration.
